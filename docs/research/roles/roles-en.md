# Who does what: the people you elect

A plain guide to what each elected office in Kenya must do, who delivers everyday services such as roads, schools and water, and what each office can and cannot do about the money. It is written for the "Who does what" page of the Kenya Electoral Map.

Facts last checked: 27 November 2026. Laws and court cases change. Check the sources before you rely on a fact.

## How to read this guide

- The Constitution of Kenya, 2010 is the main source. It is cited next to each statement in short form, for example (Constitution, Art. 185(3)) or (Fourth Schedule, Part 2, para 4).
- Acts and court rulings are used only where the Constitution does not cover the point, or where the detail comes from the Act. They are named in the same way, for example (County Governments Act, s. 9(2)).
- The full source, link and check status for each fact is in `facts.csv`. The topic-by-topic table is in `services.csv`.
- Each fact is marked verified, partial or unverified in `facts.csv`.
  - Verified: we read the text of the Constitution or Act and it says this.
  - Partial: we read the text, but of an older edition, or we checked the case only through news reports.
  - Unverified: we found it reported but could not read the source.
- Where the sources disagree or the law is being argued in court, this guide says "not settled".
- We did not read the Standing Orders of either House (see section 8).

Short names for sources:

- Constitution = Constitution of Kenya, 2010. https://www.parliament.go.ke/sites/default/files/2023-03/The_Constitution_of_Kenya_2010.pdf
- Fourth Schedule = the Constitution's list of which government does what.
- County Governments Act = County Governments Act, 2012 (we read the edition as at 31 May 2019).
- PFM Act = Public Finance Management Act, 2012 (we read the 2012 text).
- NG-CDF Act = National Government Constituencies Development Fund Act, 2015 (we read the Revised Edition 2023).

## 1. The short version

| Seat | Who votes for it | What it is | Sits in |
|---|---|---|---|
| President | Whole country | Head of State and Government. Leads the national government. (Constitution, Art. 131(1)) | The national executive (the Cabinet) |
| Governor | One county | Chief executive of the county. Leads the county government. (Constitution, Art. 179(4)) | The county executive committee |
| Senator | One county | Speaks for the county and protects its interests. (Constitution, Art. 96(1)) | The Senate |
| Woman Representative | One county | A member of the National Assembly elected by all voters in the county. (Constitution, Art. 97(1)(b)) | The National Assembly |
| MP | One constituency | Speaks for the constituency, makes national laws, approves the national budget. (Constitution, Art. 95) | The National Assembly |
| MCA | One ward | Speaks for the ward, makes county laws, approves the county budget, checks the county executive. (Constitution, Art. 185; County Governments Act, s. 8, s. 9) | The county assembly |

Deputies are not on the ballot as separate seats. The President, Deputy President, Governor and deputy governor each run as a pair. (Constitution, Art. 148(1), Art. 180(5)-(6))

## 2. Two governments, and who does what

- Kenya has a national government and 47 county governments. They are separate and depend on each other. (Constitution, Art. 6(2))
- The Fourth Schedule lists the job of each. Part 1 is national. Part 2 is county. (Constitution, Art. 186(1))
- A job the Constitution or a national law does not give to a county is a national job. (Constitution, Art. 186(3))
- A job can move from one government to the other by agreement, with the money to do it. But the legal responsibility stays with the government named in the Fourth Schedule. (Constitution, Art. 187(1)-(2))
- The two governments must respect each other's work and cooperate. (Constitution, Art. 189(1)-(2))

The people you elect do not all sit in the same government:

- National government: the President (and the Deputy President). Parliament, which is the National Assembly (MPs, Woman Reps) and the Senate, makes laws and checks the national government. (Constitution, Art. 94(1), Art. 95, Art. 96)
- County government: the Governor (and deputy) lead the executive. MCAs sit in the county assembly, which makes county laws and checks the executive. (Constitution, Art. 176(1), Art. 185)

## 3. The offices, one by one

### President and Deputy President

What they do:
- The President is Head of State and Government, leads the national executive, is Commander-in-Chief of the Defence Forces and chairs the National Security Council. (Constitution, Art. 131(1))
- The President names Cabinet Secretaries and other senior officers, and they take office with the National Assembly's approval. The President chairs Cabinet and directs ministries and government departments. (Constitution, Art. 132(2)-(3))
- The Deputy President is the President's main assistant and does the work the Constitution or the President assigns. (Constitution, Art. 147(1)-(2))

What they do not do:
- They do not run county services. Those belong to county governments. (Fourth Schedule, Part 2)
- The President cannot give orders to the Inspector-General of Police about a particular case or a particular person. (Constitution, Art. 245(4))

Who checks them: Cabinet Secretaries must come to a committee of the National Assembly or the Senate and answer questions when asked. (Constitution, Art. 153(3)) The National Assembly reviews the conduct of the President and Deputy President and starts the process of removing them. The Senate decides on a removal resolution. (Constitution, Art. 95(5), Art. 96(4))

### Governor and deputy governor

What they do:
- The county's executive power is held by the county executive committee: the governor, the deputy governor, and members the governor names with the county assembly's approval. (Constitution, Art. 179(1)-(2))
- The governor and deputy are the county's chief executive and deputy chief executive. (Constitution, Art. 179(4))
- The executive committee carries out county laws, carries out national laws in the county where they require it, and manages the county administration and its departments. (Constitution, Art. 183(1))
- The executive committee may draft laws for the county assembly and must report to it regularly. (Constitution, Art. 183(2)-(3))
- The governor also represents the county, submits county plans and policies to the assembly, signs bills the assembly passes, chairs the executive committee and gives a yearly state of the county address. (County Governments Act, s. 30(2))
- The county department that runs roads, health, markets, water, nursery schools and so on works under the governor. (Fourth Schedule, Part 2; Constitution, Art. 183(1))
- The deputy governor takes over for the rest of the term if the governor leaves office. (Constitution, Art. 182(2)) The deputy governor otherwise stands in for the governor and may be given other portfolios. (County Governments Act, s. 32(2))

How a governor is elected: by the voters of the county, together with the deputy governor they named, on the same day as the general election. A person can serve at most two terms. (Constitution, Art. 180(1), (5)-(7))

What they do not do:
- They do not make county laws. The county assembly does. (Constitution, Art. 185(1))
- They do not run primary or secondary schools, national highways, the police or the courts. Those are national. (Fourth Schedule, Part 1, para 7, 8, 16, 18)
- They do not control the county budget alone. The county assembly must approve it, and the Controller of Budget approves money leaving public funds. (County Governments Act, s. 8(1)(c); PFM Act, s. 131(1); Constitution, Art. 228(4))

Who checks them: the county assembly. It can summon any person to give evidence. (Constitution, Art. 185(3), Art. 195(1)) A governor can be removed for the reasons listed in the Constitution, such as breaking the law or abusing office. (Constitution, Art. 181(1)) The process starts in the county assembly and ends in the Senate. (County Governments Act, s. 33)

### Senator

What they do:
- Represents the county and protects the interests of the county and its government. (Constitution, Art. 96(1))
- Takes part in making laws on matters that concern counties, by considering, debating and approving the Bills. (Constitution, Art. 96(2))
- Decides how national money is shared among counties. (Constitution, Art. 96(3), Art. 217)
- Oversees national money that is given to county governments. (Constitution, Art. 96(3))
- Takes part in deciding whether to remove the President or Deputy President. (Constitution, Art. 96(4))
- Hears the case if a county assembly votes to remove a governor, and a majority of all Senators must vote to uphold a charge for the governor to leave office. (County Governments Act, s. 33(3)-(7))
- The Senate and its committees can summon any person to give evidence. (Constitution, Art. 125(1))

What they do not do:
- They do not make county laws. The county assembly does. (Constitution, Art. 185(1))
- They do not run county services. (Constitution, Art. 179(1), Art. 183(1))

Not settled: the County Governments Act says the county's Senator chairs a "County Development Board" that includes the governor, MPs and the Woman Representative. It is a forum for consultation and gives views on county plans and budgets. (County Governments Act, s. 91A) This section was challenged in the High Court in 2015, and we could not open the judgment or confirm the outcome. We do not list it as a settled duty.

Nominated Senators: 20 members who are not directly elected: 16 women nominated by parties, 2 youth members and 2 members representing persons with disabilities. (Constitution, Art. 98(1)(b)-(d))

### Woman Representative (County Woman Member)

What they do:
- Elected by all registered voters in the county. Each county has one. (Constitution, Art. 97(1)(b))
- A full member of the National Assembly. So the work is the same as an MP's: speak for the people, make national laws, approve national spending and check how national money is used. (Constitution, Art. 95(1)-(4))
- Sits with the county's MPs on the County Development Board if that section stands. (County Governments Act, s. 91A(1)(c))

What they do not do:
- They do not make county laws. They do not run county services. (Constitution, Art. 185(1), Art. 183(1))

The NGAAF affirmative action fund: many people say the Woman Representative runs it. The regulations we read (2016 Treasury copy) set up a county committee of people nominated from each constituency, plus national officers. They do not list the Woman Representative as a member, and the committee chooses its own chair. (PFM (NGAAF) Regulations 2016, reg. 16). This is partial: the copy we read is marked "Revised" and its legal notice number is blank. Not settled.

### Member of Parliament (MP, for a constituency)

What they do:
- Speaks for the people of the constituency in the National Assembly. (Constitution, Art. 95(1))
- Takes part in debate and passes national laws. (Constitution, Art. 95(2)-(3))
- Votes on how national revenue is split between national and county government, approves national spending, and checks how national money is used. (Constitution, Art. 95(4))
- Reviews the conduct of the President, Deputy President and other State officers, and checks State organs. (Constitution, Art. 95(5))
- Committees can summon any person. (Constitution, Art. 125(1))
- Parliament must meet in the open and let the public take part. You can petition Parliament about any law. (Constitution, Art. 118(1), Art. 119(1))

What they do not do:
- Build roads, run schools or run hospitals. Those are jobs of the national or county executive. (Constitution, Art. 94(1); Fourth Schedule)
- Make county laws. (Constitution, Art. 185(1))

Money: the constituency fund, NG-CDF, is for projects in the constituency. It is run by a Board and a constituency committee, not by the MP. See section 5. (NG-CDF Act, s. 4, s. 43)

Nominated MPs: 12 members nominated by parliamentary parties to speak for special interests such as youth, persons with disabilities and workers. (Constitution, Art. 97(1)(c))

### Member of County Assembly (MCA, for a ward)

What they do:
- The county assembly makes the county's laws. (Constitution, Art. 185(1)-(2))
- It oversees the county executive and other county organs. (Constitution, Art. 185(3))
- It approves plans and policies for the county's resources, infrastructure and institutions. (Constitution, Art. 185(4))
- It approves the county budget and spending, county borrowing and county development plans, and vets nominees to county posts. (County Governments Act, s. 8(1))
- An MCA must keep close contact with voters, bring their views to the assembly, attend sessions, and link the assembly and the people on public service delivery. (County Governments Act, s. 9(1))
- The assembly must sit in public and let the public take part. (Constitution, Art. 196(1)) It can summon any person to give evidence. (Constitution, Art. 195(1))

What they do not do:
- An MCA "shall not be directly or indirectly involved in the executive functions of the county government" or in "the delivery of services as if the member were an officer or employee of the county government". (County Governments Act, s. 9(2))
- A ward administrator, who is a county official, runs day-to-day administration in the ward. The MCA does not. (County Governments Act, s. 51(1)-(3))

Nominated MCAs: members nominated by parties so that no more than two-thirds of the assembly are of one gender, and to include marginalised groups such as persons with disabilities and youth. (Constitution, Art. 177(1)(b)-(c))

More detail on the topics people ask about most:

**Roads.** National trunk roads and national standards for roads are national. County roads are county. (Fourth Schedule, Part 1, para 18(b),(c); Part 2, para 5(a)) In practice, national road agencies (KeNHA, KURA, KeRRA) also run roads. We could not read the Kenya Roads Act, so who looks after a given rural or urban road is "not settled" for this guide (unverified). The share of the road maintenance fuel levy for counties is also before Parliament (Roads Act (Amendment) Bill 2025, as reported 11 May 2026). Ask the county roads office or the road agency.

**Schools.** Primary schools, secondary schools and special education are national. Nursery schools, village polytechnics and childcare are county. (Fourth Schedule, Part 1, para 16; Part 2, para 9) Every child has the right to free and compulsory basic education. (Constitution, Art. 53(1)(b)) County governments pay for the buildings and equipment of nursery schools and similar centres. (Basic Education Act, s. 26(1), 2013 text, partial) The national government may hand school building work to a county by agreement. (Basic Education Act, s. 26(2); Constitution, Art. 187)

**Water.** The Constitution gives every person the right to clean and safe water in enough quantity. (Constitution, Art. 43(1)(d)) Water and sewerage services are county. Water protection and dam safety are national. (Fourth Schedule, Part 2, para 11(b); Part 1, para 22(c)) Each county must set up water service providers, and they run the water services in their area. (Water Act 2016, s. 77-78, partial)

**Health.** County health facilities, ambulances and primary health care are county. National referral hospitals and health policy are national. (Fourth Schedule, Part 2, para 2; Part 1, para 23, 28) Everyone has the right to the highest attainable standard of health. (Constitution, Art. 43(1)(a))

**Police and security.** Police services are national. (Fourth Schedule, Part 1, para 7) The Inspector-General has independent command, and nobody can direct the Inspector-General on a particular case. (Constitution, Art. 245(2)(b), (4)) Each county has a County Policing Authority, chaired by the Governor or an executive member the Governor appoints. It proposes police priorities for the county. The law does not let it interfere with a particular investigation or operation. (National Police Service Act, s. 41, 2014 text, partial)

**Land.** Public land is held by the county in trust for residents, or by the national government for the people of Kenya, and the National Land Commission administers it. (Constitution, Art. 62(2)-(3), Art. 67(2)(a)) County governments handle county land survey, boundaries and fencing. (Fourth Schedule, Part 2, para 8) We did not read the land registration laws, so who registers a title is not covered here.

**Courts.** Courts are national and independent. (Fourth Schedule, Part 1, para 8; Constitution, Art. 159(1))

## 5. Money each office influences

### County budget (MCAs approve, the executive spends)

- The county executive committee member for finance gives the budget estimates to the executive committee, then to the county assembly. (PFM Act, s. 129, partial)
- The county assembly considers the estimates and approves them, with or without changes, so the budget law passes by 30 June. A committee reviews them first and must consider the public's views. (PFM Act, s. 131(1)-(2); County Governments Act, s. 8(1)(c))
- Changes must follow the county fiscal strategy paper, and any increase must be balanced by a cut elsewhere. (PFM Act, s. 131(3))
- No county money may be spent outside a plan approved by the county assembly. (County Governments Act, s. 104(1))
- The Controller of Budget must authorise money coming out of public funds, and may only do so if the law allows it. (Constitution, Art. 228(4)-(5))
- The public has a right to take part in money matters. (Constitution, Art. 201(a); County Governments Act, s. 87, s. 91)

### National budget and sharing money among counties

- MPs approve national spending. (Constitution, Art. 95(4), Art. 221(6))
- Each year Parliament must receive a bill that splits revenue between national and county government, and a bill that splits the county share among the counties. (Constitution, Art. 218(1))
- Every five years the Senate decides the basis for sharing the county level's money among counties. It takes advice, consults governors and the Treasury, and invites public views. (Constitution, Art. 217(1)-(2))
- A county's share must be sent to it without undue delay and without deductions. (Constitution, Art. 219)

### NG-CDF (the constituency fund)

What it is: a national government fund of at least 2.5 per cent of the national government's share of revenue. A Board runs it. (NG-CDF Act, s. 4(1), Rev. 2023)

What it can pay for: only works and services that are national government functions. (NG-CDF Act, s. 24, Rev. 2023) Schools are a national function, so classrooms qualify. County roads, nursery schools and water supply are county functions, so on the wording of the Act they should not qualify. We did not check how this works in practice.

Who decides: each constituency has a committee of a national government official, three men, three women (including a youth of each sex), one person with a disability, a Board officer and one co-opted member. The 2023 text does not list the MP. (NG-CDF Act, s. 43(2), Rev. 2023) The committee chair holds open ward forums in the first year of a new Parliament and at least every two years after that, and the committee chooses priority projects. (NG-CDF Act, s. 27)

Bursaries: a committee may use the fund for education bursaries, with Board approval. (NG-CDF Act, s. 48A)

Who checks it: the National Assembly must have a select committee on the fund. (NG-CDF Act, s. 50)

Limits: it may not be used to support political bodies or political activities, or religious bodies or activities (a narrow exception for emergency support exists). (NG-CDF Act, s. 25(3)-(4))

Legal status, as of 27 November 2026 (not settled):

- August 2022: the Supreme Court struck down the earlier CDF Act of 2013 for breaching separation of powers. (Institute for Social Accountability v National Assembly, [2022] KESC 39; partial)
- 20 September 2024: the High Court declared the 2015 Act (as amended in 2022 and 2023) unconstitutional and set 30 June 2026 for the fund to stop. (Gikonyo v National Assembly, [2024] KEHC 10886; partial)
- 6 February 2026: the Court of Appeal set that judgment aside and upheld the Act, except subsection 43(9), which it found unconstitutional. The fund continues. (Civil Appeal E884 of 2024; partial, from news reports)
- 6 February 2026: the petitioners gave notice that they will go to the Supreme Court. We found no Supreme Court judgment on the main case. A ruling on an application in the case, dated 25 September 2026, is listed but we could not read it. (unverified)
- 1 July 2025: the National Assembly passed a Constitution amendment Bill (National Assembly Bill No. 4 of 2025) to write the NG-CDF, a Senate Oversight Fund and the NGAAF into the Constitution. It needs the Senate. We found no record of Senate action. (partial)

### NGAAF

The National Government Affirmative Action Fund supports women, youth, persons with disabilities, children and the elderly. (PFM (NGAAF) Regulations 2016, reg. 2; partial) It is a national fund run under Treasury regulations, with a county committee. We could not confirm a role for the Woman Representative from the regulations. Its legal base is described differently in different sources, and a 2026 parliamentary committee recommended moving it into an Act. (unverified)

### Ward development funds

Some counties have county laws that set up a ward development fund. These are different in each county, and their legality has been questioned. The County Governments Act says an MCA must stay out of executive work and service delivery. (County Governments Act, s. 9(2)) We did not find or read a court judgment on them, so this is not settled and unverified. Ask your county assembly for the county's law.

### County Development Board (as described in the Act)

See the Senator section above. Not settled.

## 6. Common myths

| What people say | What the law says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "The MP builds roads." | National roads are built by the national government. County roads are built by the county government. MPs make laws, approve the national budget and check national spending. | Fourth Schedule, Part 1, para 18; Part 2, para 5; Constitution, Art. 95 |
| "The MP can fund anything in the constituency." | NG-CDF may only fund projects that are national government functions. A committee chooses them, not the MP. | NG-CDF Act, s. 24, s. 43(2) |
| "The MCA pays school fees." | An MCA does not deliver services. Every child has the right to free and compulsory basic education. Bursaries come from the NG-CDF committee or from a county scheme if the county has one. | County Governments Act, s. 9(2); Constitution, Art. 53(1)(b); NG-CDF Act, s. 48A |
| "The MCA fixes the road or the street light in the ward." | The county executive does, under the Governor. The MCA votes on the budget and checks that it is spent. | Fourth Schedule, Part 2, para 5; Constitution, Art. 185(3); County Governments Act, s. 9(2) |
| "The Governor runs the primary and secondary schools." | They are national. The county runs nursery schools. | Fourth Schedule, Part 1, para 16; Part 2, para 9 |
| "The Governor controls the police." | Police are national and the Inspector-General has independent command. The Governor chairs the County Policing Authority, which proposes priorities but cannot direct operations. | Fourth Schedule, Part 1, para 7; Constitution, Art. 245; National Police Service Act, s. 41 |
| "The Senator is the county's top lawmaker for county laws." | County laws are made by the county assembly. The Senate speaks for counties in national law and decides how money is shared. | Constitution, Art. 185(1); Art. 96 |
| "The Woman Rep only represents women." | She is elected by all voters in the county and is a full member of the National Assembly. | Constitution, Art. 97(1)(b), Art. 95 |
| "The Governor sets the county budget alone." | The executive prepares it, the county assembly approves it, and the Controller of Budget authorises withdrawals. | PFM Act, s. 129, s. 131; Constitution, Art. 228(4) |
| "The MP can remove the Governor." | The county assembly starts the process and the Senate decides. | County Governments Act, s. 33; Constitution, Art. 181 |
| "The county owns and sells public land." | The county holds public land in trust for residents, and the National Land Commission administers it. | Constitution, Art. 62(2), Art. 67(2)(a) |
| "NG-CDF has been abolished." | Not as of 27 November 2026. The Court of Appeal restored the Act on 6 February 2026, and the case may go to the Supreme Court. | See section 5 (partial) |
| "Every county has a ward fund run by the MCA." | Only some counties have one, set by county law, and the legality is questioned. | See section 5 (unverified) |

## 7. How to take part

- Parliament must meet in public and let you take part. You can petition it. (Constitution, Art. 118(1), Art. 119(1))
- Your county assembly must sit in public and let you take part. (Constitution, Art. 196(1))
- Your county must set up ways for you to take part, such as town hall meetings and budget forums, and you can petition the county government in writing. (County Governments Act, s. 88, s. 91; partial)
- Every citizen has the right to ask for information held by the State. (Constitution, Art. 35(1)(a))
